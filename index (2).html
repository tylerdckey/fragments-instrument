Catch a moment when it grabs you. Mark what you can. Read the pattern later — not on a clock.

this stretch

Seven days. A memory aid, not a pattern — a week can't carry one, and reading it as though it could is reading noise. It's here so a stretch close is written off what happened rather than what you remember, and so a quiet week is visible while you can still do something about it.

The same three the estuarine map uses for its actants, so the two instruments can be read against each other directly. Three legitimate answers and no obviously right one — which is also what stops it being answered flatteringly.

08 · explicabilityCould I explain this to someone?

The moments you can't yet explain tend to matter most. Rate it honestly rather than usefully — the read surfaces the edge four separate ways, so nothing is gained by pushing this one.

09 · wholesWhich wholes did this serve? Weight any that apply.

Not a choice between them — most moments serve several at once, at different weights. Leave any at zero. Replaces the old three-way pick, which forced them to compete.

10 · dispositionMore moments like this, or fewer?

Your own flag, in your own reckoning. Separate from the sliders: it surfaces the moments you marked, whatever the pattern says.

11 · the forward read optional, and only now and then
In a word or a hashtag, what does this make you expect?
From here, the future feels—

The rest of this form reads what happened. Nothing else in the practice signifies forward, which is the layer direction is supposed to come from. Signify an imagined future — you don't have to narrate one. Asked every time it becomes a demand, so it's folded away by default.

What this instrument is — and isn't

This borrows Snowden's SenseMaker grammar — an open prompt, then you signify your own story with triads and sliders rather than interpreting it — and bends it to a single life. That bend has a cost. With one person signifying, self-interpretation can't correct your bias the way it does across a crowd. The repair is a real person: have someone you trust place a few of your fragments in their own words first, then look at where their reading diverges from yours.

No schedule, no streak, no quota. Capture when a moment grabs you — five some days, none on others; zero isn't a lapse. Form stays the same every time (that's the calm part); when and how often is entirely yours. This is externalised memory, an accommodation — you don't have to hold the shape of your life in working memory; the instrument holds it and shows you later.

The Read tab is gated behind a written prediction — best done when a pile has built up or you feel foggy, not daily. You're hoping to be surprised; if every read confirms your guess, the prompt is too leading. Agency and service are triads, not sliders, because each holds three real things that don't reduce to a line. The ★ this matters flag is your own signal, surfaced on its own. The Read tab also shows a vector — which way the good moments are drifting — as a direction to notice, never a score to chase. The grammar is Snowden's; treating your own moments as the population, the prediction gate, the salience flag, the vector read, and the skip-friendly framing are a synthesis on top of his method.

Before you look: write what you expect to see. Being wrong is the result worth having — if the pattern matches your guess exactly, distrust it.

4The tail — four channels, read together

Four independent ways a moment can be at the edge. Where they agree is the strongest signal you have; where they disagree is worth a second look. The centre told you what you knew, this is where the new thing is.

5What the crossings say

Modifiers earn their place here. Two cells matter more than the rest: felt bad but gave more than it took is growth — hard and generative, and easy to avoid. Felt good but took more than it gave is the comfort trap. Neither is visible without crossing the two.

10Close the read

A read that leaves nothing behind can't teach you anything. This is where the archetypes get chosen and the conditions get named — and where, over several reads, you find out whether your predictions are getting better. That series is the only real measure of whether this instrument is worth running.

Edit the signifiers to fit your life. Any change stamps a new version, so old fragments keep their original meaning and the read can hold the eras apart. Don't tune often — a moving ruler measures nothing.
